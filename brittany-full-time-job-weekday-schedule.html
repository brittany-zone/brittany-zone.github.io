Owning a Brittany With a Full-Time Job: A Realistic Weekday Exercise and Training Schedule

Before work, midday and evening: a weekday plan built around your own clock, with a planner to test it.

Owning a Brittany with a full-time job is possible, but not on autopilot. The breed was built to hunt for hours, and a standard office day leaves a dog alone about twice as long as welfare charities advise. This guide turns that gap into a realistic weekday exercise and training schedule: what to do before work, how to break up the middle of the day, where five-minute training blocks fit, and how to plan it around your own clock instead of someone else's.

Quick answer: can you own a Brittany with a full-time job?

Yes, if three things are true on most weekdays: your Brittany gets a real outing before you leave, the workday is broken by a midday visit or company, and the evening includes both running and a short training session. Without the midday break, an eight-hour shift plus commute is longer than the published guidance supports.

How much weekday exercise does a Brittany need?

Plan for 60 to 120 minutes of combined physical and mental activity on a workday, and more on days off. Published figures differ, so treat them as a range to fit your own dog into, not as a single pass mark.

What should the morning before work look like?

The morning outing is the one you cannot skip, because everything your dog does while you are away depends on it. Aim for 30 to 45 minutes outside that mixes free movement, sniffing and a few minutes of training, and be back with time to spare.

Finish half an hour before you leave

The RSPCA's advice is specific about timing: exercise your dog before leaving, and return home half an hour before you are due to go. It also suggests feeding a small meal shortly before leaving and making sure your dog has been to the toilet. For a Brittany that buffer matters. A bird dog that walks back through the door and watches you grab your keys two minutes later is still in hunting gear; one that has had 30 minutes to drink, eat and lie down is far more likely to sleep.

Make the morning about the nose, not only the legs

You will not out-run a Brittany before 8 a.m., and you do not need to. Let the first ten minutes be a loose sniffing walk, use the middle for whatever running your area allows (a long line in a field, a fenced park, a fetch game), and finish with five minutes of calm work such as recall or heel on the way home. If your dog locks onto birds on the way, the routine in our guide to teaching a walk-away cue when a Brittany points on leash keeps the walk moving without a tug-of-war.

30 min

The wind-down gap between the end of the morning walk and your departure, as suggested by the RSPCA. Count backwards from the time you leave to find your real wake-up time.

How long can a Brittany be left alone during a workday?

Plan around four hours at a stretch. That is the general figure the RSPCA gives for dogs, and the Brittany is not a breed that scores well on solitude, so the middle of the workday needs a visit, company or a change of plan.

The RSPCA's guide to training a dog to be left alone says the length of time depends on the individual dog, but advises "no longer than four hours" so that dogs have opportunities to exercise, go to the toilet and spend time with people. It also describes building alone time gradually, and says that once a dog is happy to be left for up to an hour, longer periods should follow without problems.

What Brittany owners actually do

Real households are messier than guidelines. In a June 2026 r/BrittanySpaniel thread about a 14-month-old Brittany facing eight-hour days, the replies split. Some owners reported adult Brittanys that free-roam the house and mostly sleep through a full shift. Others called eight crated hours too long for a young dog and urged paying for a midday walk. One reply laid out the middle ground: a hard run in the morning, a pen in a big room rather than a crate, frozen food toys, and a neighbor to let the dog out at midday. Another said that if nothing else is possible, paying a dog walker for a 20-minute midday walk "would be better than nothing". These are owner reports, not research, but they show the same pattern as the official advice: the dogs that cope have a break or a lot of morning exercise, usually both.

Setting up the hours in between

The RSPCA suggests leaving a "special" toy that only comes out when you go, such as a rubber toy stuffed with food or a treat ball filled with dry food, and taking those treats out of the daily food allowance. Royal Canin makes the same point about rewards coming from the daily kibble portion. In practice that means part of breakfast goes into the food toy instead of the bowl.

What does a realistic weekday schedule look like?

A realistic weekday has three anchors (morning outing, midday break, evening outing) and three five-minute training blocks. The sample below assumes you leave at 8:00, get home at 17:30 and have a 20-minute midday visit.

Where does training fit on a workday?

Training fits in five-minute blocks attached to things you already do: the end of the morning walk, the evening outing and dinner. A Brittany does not need an hour-long lesson after work; it needs short, frequent work for its brain.

Royal Canin describes the Brittany as a willing student and a quick learner, and adds that a gentle approach works best because the breed does not respond well to harsh methods. That suits a tired owner: three calm five-minute sessions are easier to keep up than one long one.

What should the evening look like?

The evening is for the day's biggest outing first and calm second. Go out soon after you get home, before you sit down, and give your Brittany the chance to run that the morning could not offer.

After 35 to 60 minutes outside, most of the remaining work is teaching your dog that the day is over. A Brittany that paces and brings toys at 9 p.m. after a long outing is usually short of an off switch, not short of miles; our step-by-step guide on teaching an off switch to a Brittany that is still wired after a walk covers that skill. Resist the urge to add a second hard session every time your dog looks restless. You would be building fitness and expectation, not calm.

Common mistakes when owning a Brittany with a full-time job

  • Saving all the exercise for the evening. The dog then spends the whole workday with a full tank. Move at least a third of the minutes to the morning.
  • Walking in the door from the morning outing and leaving straight away. Keep the half-hour wind-down the RSPCA suggests.
  • Counting only miles. The published range includes mental work. Ten minutes of nose work can replace a slice of pavement walking on a rainy Tuesday.
  • Assuming a yard replaces the midday break. A Brittany alone in a yard is still alone, and a bird dog with a fence to test is not resting.
  • Making weekends wildly different. A four-hour hike on Saturday is great; skipping the Monday morning outing because of it is not.
  • Copying another household's schedule. A 10-year-old Brittany that naps through a shift tells you little about a 14-month-old. Build the plan from your own dog's behavior.

How this schedule was put together

The figures in this guide come from four published sources: the Royal Kennel Club's Brittany breed page (exercise band, home type), Royal Canin's Brittany profile (daily range, alone-time score, training approach), the RSPCA's guide to leaving a dog alone (four-hour figure, timing of the morning walk, food toys) and a June 2026 owner discussion on r/BrittanySpaniel (what working owners report). The breed figures are general guidance, not measurements of individual dogs, and the two exercise figures do not agree with each other, which is why the article works with a range. The clock times in the sample schedule and the planner are this guide's own layout built on those figures. No survey has measured how Brittanys in full-time working households actually spend their weekdays, so that part rests on owner reports.

FAQ: Brittany and a full-time job

Can a Brittany be left alone for 8 hours while I work?

Not as a daily routine without a break. The RSPCA advises leaving a dog alone for no longer than four hours, and Royal Canin scores the Brittany 1 out of 5 for staying alone. Split the day with a midday visit, a lunch trip home or daycare.

How much exercise does a Brittany need on a workday?

Plan for 60 to 120 minutes of combined physical and mental activity. Royal Canin gives one to two hours a day including mental stimulation; the Royal Kennel Club lists the breed in its "more than 2 hours per day" band, which is easier to reach on days off.

How long should I walk my Brittany before work?

Aim for 30 to 45 minutes that mixes sniffing, some running and a few minutes of training, and finish about half an hour before you leave, as the RSPCA suggests, so your dog can eat, drink and settle.

Is a dog walker enough for a Brittany?

A midday walker solves the alone-time problem, not the whole exercise need. Count the visit as 20 to 45 minutes of the daily total and keep your own morning and evening outings.

Should I crate my Brittany while I am at work?

Owners disagree. In the r/BrittanySpaniel discussion quoted above, several people preferred a pen or dog-proofed room for a full shift and kept the crate for shorter periods. Whatever space you use, the midday break still applies.

Do scent games and training count as exercise?

Yes, toward the daily total. Royal Canin's one-to-two-hour range for the Brittany explicitly includes mental stimulation, so three five-minute training or nose-work blocks are part of the plan, not an extra.

Is a Brittany a good dog for someone who works full time?

It can be for an active owner with a midday solution and reliable mornings and evenings. It is a poor match if the dog would be alone for a full shift every day with no break and only a short walk afterward.

Your weekday, your Brittany

Owning a Brittany with a full-time job comes down to a realistic weekday exercise and training schedule that you can repeat on a wet Wednesday: an outing that ends half an hour before you leave, a break near the middle of the day, the big run after work and three short training blocks. Build it from your own times with the planner, test it for two weeks and change one block at a time. A plan you keep at 90 minutes beats a perfect one you drop by Thursday.
