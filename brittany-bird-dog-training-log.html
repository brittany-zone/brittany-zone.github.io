In this log

    Print at 100% scale (“actual size”). A pencil works best afield, even when the page gets damp.

    What each page holds

    The cover page holds the dog’s name, the season, an “if found” box, the drills for the season with room to write what “done” looks like, and a key for your own cues and whistle signals. Every session sheet repeats the same fields: date, place and time, cover, weather, a compass to circle the wind, a tally for finds, points, flushes and retrieves, your drills rated 1 to 5, notes, and one line for what to work on next time. The season summary is a grid of sessions and drills, followed by a page of first times.

    Using the log afield

    • Fill it in before you drive home. Two minutes at the end of the session, while it is fresh.
    • Rate the dog, not the day. A 3 on a windy day with wild birds can be better work than a 5 on planted birds.
    • Change one thing at a time. The “next time” line keeps the next session focused.
    • Copy the ratings to the summary. Once a week is enough to see which drills are rising and which are stuck.

    Why a log for a Brittany

    The AKC breed page calls the Brittany an agile, energetic and versatile gundog that is “tireless afield”, and lists field trials among the sports this trainable breed takes to. A dog with that much drive changes quickly, so small written notes make the progress easier to see than memory alone.
